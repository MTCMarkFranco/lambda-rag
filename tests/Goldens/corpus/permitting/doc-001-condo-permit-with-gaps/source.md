# Building Permit Application — 245 Bay Tower, Toronto, ON

A 38-storey residential condominium tower with two levels of underground
parking and street-level commercial. This permit application is filed
with the City of Toronto and addresses Ontario Building Code, applicable
zoning, and the related supporting studies.

## Project Description

The proposal is a 38-storey, 312-unit residential condominium with
mixed-use commercial at grade. Construction type is concrete frame on
caisson foundations, with two levels of below-grade parking.

## Zoning District

The site is located in CR3.0 (C2.0; R2.5) SS2 (x123) per Toronto Zoning
By-law 569-2013. The proposed use is a permitted use in the CR
zoning district. A minor variance is not required.

## Setbacks

- Front yard setback (Bay Street): 4.5 m
- Rear yard setback: 7.5 m
- Side yard (north): 3.0 m
- Side yard (south): 3.0 m

All setbacks meet or exceed the minimum setbacks prescribed by By-law
569-2013, Section 40.10.40.

## Building Height

The proposed building height is 122.0 m to the top of the mechanical
penthouse, complying with the maximum height permitted in the CR zoning
district under By-law 569-2013.

## Fire Egress

Two scissor stairs provide independent means of egress from each floor.
The travel distance from the most remote point on each floor to the
nearest exit door is 24.0 m. The building is fully sprinklered.

## Fire Suppression — Sprinkler System

A wet-pipe sprinkler system is provided throughout the building.
Standpipes are provided in each exit stair. The fire alarm system is
two-stage, supervised by a ULC-listed monitoring service.

## Stormwater Management

A stormwater management plan has been prepared in accordance with the
Toronto Wet Weather Flow Master Plan, including a green roof (35% of
roof area) and an underground detention tank sized for the 100-year
storm. Low impact development measures include permeable pavers in the
loading area.

## Heritage Preservation

The site contains a designated heritage property (245 Bay) under Part
IV of the Ontario Heritage Act. A Heritage Impact Assessment has been
prepared in accordance with City of Toronto guidelines, and the façade
of the heritage building will be retained and integrated into the new
construction.

## Traffic Impact Study

A traffic impact study (TIS) has been prepared by a qualified
transportation engineer. Background traffic counts were taken at each
adjacent intersection during the AM and PM peak. The study concludes
that the development will not degrade the level of service (LOS) at any
adjacent intersection below LOS D.
