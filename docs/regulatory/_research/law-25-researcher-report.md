It’s unclear to what extent human involvement disqualifies a decision from being “exclusively” automated.
If an AI provides a recommendation but a human rubber-stamps it 99% of the time, is that considered automated or human? The CAI has not fully clarified how minimal human oversight might remove an obligation. For now, prudent approach is to treat any decision with
as subject to Art 12.1 requirements.
The definition of what constitutes a transfer “outside Québec” can be debated. For example, if a Quebec resident’s data is stored on a cloud server physically in Ontario but managed by a Quebec-based company, is that a transfer that needs an assessment? Probably yes, since physically outside. Another grey zone:
(like an employee on vacation accessing data) – likely not considered a transfer in the legal sense. Guidance is limited; organizations often err on the side of performing TIAs for any routine out-of-province storage.
In public bodies, the interplay between Law 25 updates and existing
or sector-specific rules can be confusing. E.g., can a citizen ask a ministry to delete their data under “cessation de diffusion”? Likely no if the data is part of an official archive or public register (because then exceptions for archival/public interest apply). These
